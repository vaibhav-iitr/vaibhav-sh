Agent OS

A working set of AI agents one operator uses every day.
Not a framework. Not a library. Not a product.

Running anything serious solo means losing the parts of work that a good team gives you. Not just hands. Someone who keeps the board, someone who pushes back on a half-baked decision, someone who notices that the same problem has come up three weeks in a row. Those are not assistant tasks. They are colleague tasks.

The premise behind this set is that a well-instructed agent, given a clear role and a stable protocol, can hold a meaningful slice of that colleague work. Each agent has a name and a persona — borrowed from television and film — because a persona is a compression algorithm for tone. Naming an agent "Research Agent" produces flat output. The persona is the highest-leverage piece of instruction in any agent's protocol.

The agent protocols shipped to GitHub in May 2026. Two weeks later, a Slack listener went on top: a persistent daemon that watches a private channel, resolves an agent name from the mention, fires a Claude session in that agent's working directory, and posts the result back in-thread. The command is @Agent OS captain run a 12-role debate on this. It works from a phone on a train. The runtime layer ships to the public repo in June 2026.

The agents

Donna The keystone. Tracks every task across every agent, runs daily and weekly briefs, surfaces anything overdue, stalled, or quietly aging. Everything else hangs off her.
Captain Runs a twelve-role engineering debate before any technical decision. Architect, PM, ML scientist, QA, OpEx, launch and risk — each speaks specifically, each pushes back, the Chief breaks ties. Dissent stays visible.
Sherlock Does the same for non-engineering decisions. Frames the question, draws out the position, notes what could falsify it.
Alfred Evaluates the rest of the agents on seven dimensions and proposes amendments. Reports directly to the operator. Anyone evaluating the rest of the system needs an unfiltered line to the principal.
Roy Keeps the operating system of the agents healthy. Stale state files, broken cross-references, missing credentials — Roy notices and routes them.
Jarvis Writes a pre-meeting brief. Who is in the room, what they last talked about, what is likely to come up, what to watch for.
Emily Runs the content operation. Takes research, observations, and lived experience and turns them into the AI for PMs series on LinkedIn and Substack. A content agent whose output is publicly visible is also a test of whether the rest of the system is actually producing anything worth saying.
Oracle An honest artifact. Started as a standing research agent and was demoted to an invocation pattern in April 2026 — on-demand research is a tool you reach for, not an agent you maintain. The demotion is included because it is part of the story.

Patterns worth stealing

A debate-then-decide protocol beats a single-pass recommendation.

Captain's twelve-role structure is slower than asking one assistant the same question. It is also better. The dissent stays visible. A decision that survived an internal argument tends to survive contact with reality.

Agents that evaluate agents are non-negotiable above a certain size.

Once there are more than three or four agents in regular use, there is no way for the operator to notice the slow drift in any one of them. Alfred is the answer to that.

The persona is the highest-leverage piece of instruction.

A persona encodes tone, posture, willingness to push back. The personas are not load-bearing for adoption — swap them for whoever fits your own context. The discipline that matters is having a named, distinct voice for each role.

An always-on listener changes the dispatch economics.

The bottleneck in a file-based agent system is not the agent — it is remembering to open the tool, find the agent, and start the session. A listener that watches a Slack channel removes that friction entirely. The same protocol that worked at a desk now works on a train.
